\subsubsection{Statistics of the control campaign}
Figure~\ref{fig:X11} gives distributions rather than means. The empirical CDF of the RMS lateral error in the combined scenario shows that the NMPC is better than the PD controller in almost every realisation, with a heavy upper tail caused by the degraded-GNSS component (a \SI{1.5}{m} bias cannot be controlled away because the controller does not observe it). The worst-case NMPC solve time per run was \SI{\nsolveMax}{ms} against a \SI{100}{ms} budget; the CBF-QP adds about \SI{0.3}{ms}. The mean peak lateral error ranks the scenarios in the order of difficulty S1 $<$ S3 $<$ S2 $<$ S5 $<$ S4 $\approx$ S6.

\subsubsection{Step response}
A \SI{1}{m} lateral slot step commanded over \SI{0.4}{s} without disturbances gives the loop characteristics in Fig.~\ref{fig:X12b}: overshoot \nstepPOver\,\% for the PD law, \nstepNOver\,\% for the NMPC and \nstepCOver\,\% with the filter; $2\,\%$ settling time \nstepPSettle, \nstepNSettle{} and \nstepCSettle\,s; 10--90\,\% rise time \nstepPRise, \nstepNRise{} and \nstepCRise\,s. The bank angle never exceeds the \SI{35}{\degree} limit. The filter is inactive in this test because the barrier is far from violated.

\subsubsection{Margin ablation of the safety filter}
The HOCBF buffer $\Delta D$---the filter enforces $\|\mathbf p_{\mathrm{rel,meas}}\|\ge D_{\mathrm{safe}}+\Delta D$, while physical violations $h<0$ are always evaluated against the true hard boundary $\|\mathbf p_{\mathrm{rel}}\|<D_{\mathrm{safe}}=\SI{4.24}{m}$---trades safety for wake benefit because a larger buffer deflects the follower from the slot earlier. Figure~\ref{fig:X12}a--c shows the true violation rate, RMS lateral tracking error and mean drag-coefficient saving for all five tested margins $\Delta D\in\{0,0.5,1.0,1.5,2.0\}$\,m across all \ncbfAblSeeds\ seeds per scenario (aligning $\Delta D=\SI{0.5}{m}$ identically with the nominal \texttt{NMPC+HOCBF} column of Table~\ref{tab:control}). In the braking scenario (S3) true violations are \ncbfThreeVMzero\,\%, \ncbfThreeVMhalf\,\%, \ncbfThreeVMone\,\%, \ncbfThreeVMonehalf\,\% and \ncbfThreeVMtwo\,\% across the five margins, with mean RMS lateral error \ncbfThreeEMzero, \ncbfThreeEMhalf, \ncbfThreeEMone, \ncbfThreeEMonehalf{} and \ncbfThreeEMtwo\,m; in the combined scenario (S5) the violation rates are \ncbfFiveVMzero\,\%, \ncbfFiveVMhalf\,\%, \ncbfFiveVMone\,\%, \ncbfFiveVMonehalf\,\% and \ncbfFiveVMtwo\,\%; and in the over-capability scenario (S6) \ncbfSixVMzero\,\%, \ncbfSixVMhalf\,\%, \ncbfSixVMone\,\%, \ncbfSixVMonehalf\,\% and \ncbfSixVMtwo\,\%. The saving index (mean drag-coefficient reduction in units of $10^{-4}$) falls from \ncbfFiveSMzero{} to \ncbfFiveSMhalf, \ncbfFiveSMone, \ncbfFiveSMonehalf{} and \ncbfFiveSMtwo{} across the five margins in S5 as lateral deflection grows. In S5, the residual violating realisations combine leader braking during communication dropout with an unobservable \SI{1.5}{m} GNSS bias vector oriented so that perceived separation overstates true separation while longitudinal braking saturates. Overall, radial buffer inflation cannot substitute for previewed evasive authority: under longitudinal deceleration saturation a static radial margin leaves hard-boundary violations largely unchanged while steadily eroding the wake benefit.
